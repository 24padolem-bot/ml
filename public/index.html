Methodology, Validation Protocols & Governance

Academic rigor, zero data leakage protocols, and Viva examination preparation guide.

1. Engine-Level Group Validation (GroupKFold)

Turbofan engine data consists of multi-cycle operational time series for each unique unit. Splitting cycles at random between train and test sets introduces catastrophic target leakage. In this system, all validation splits are strictly performed by unit_nr, ensuring that validation engines are entirely unseen during training.

2. Causal Feature Engineering (Strictly Historical)

All 78 engineered features (rolling means over 5 and 15 cycles, rolling standard deviations, and lag differences) are grouped strictly by unit_nr. Feature windows never bridge across different engines, and zero future telemetry cycles are referenced.

3. RUL Piecewise Linear Capping (RUL = 125)

Turbofan engines do not exhibit measurable physical degradation during early flight cycles. Following the foundational methodology of Saxena et al. (IEEE PHM 2008), the target RUL is capped at 125 cycles to prevent regressor bias during nominal initial operation.

4. Key Academic References

  • Saxena, A., et al. (2008). Damage propagation modeling for aircraft engine run-to-failure simulation. IEEE PHM 2008. DOI: 10.1109/PHM.2008.4578638
  • Ramasso, E., & Saxena, A. (2014). Performance benchmarking of prognostic methods for turbofan engines. IJPHM, 5(2), 1-15.
  • Heimes, F. O. (2008). Recurrent neural networks for remaining useful life estimation. IEEE PHM 2008.
